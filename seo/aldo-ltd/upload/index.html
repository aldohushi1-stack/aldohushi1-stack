The magnum opus

Glassbox for Claude Code. 9/10, on the way to 10.

Every Claude Code session leaves a flight recorder behind, and almost nobody reads it. Glassbox reads it for you: where the tokens and the dollars went, what went wrong, and what to change. It is free, MIT-licensed, and nothing leaves your machine.

I built it after one of my own sessions cost nearly fifteen times what it reported. That night is below.

The mission

The last point is the hardest one.

Nine is what one person can do alone, in public, with tests. Ten is what other people can trust without having to take my word for it. Here is the road, in the open, so you can hold me to it.

  1. Done · the 9Build it properly

    Viewer, CLI, Stop hook, Claude Code plugin, redaction with keyed file names, secrets scanner, adherence check, loop guard. Tests on every release, CI green.

  2. NextA second maintainer

    The one point it lost. The project needs someone besides me who can review, release and say no.

  3. ThenAn outside security review

    Glassbox reads transcripts and hunts secrets in them. That code deserves eyes that aren't mine before anyone relies on it in production.

  4. That makes 101.0, and a promise

    Tested against every Claude Code transcript change, a written support policy, semver you can plan around. Ready for the whole ecosystem, not only early adopters.

Want to help carry it to ten? Open an issue, review a module, or write to me about maintaining it.

  • fenceFinds keys, tokens and passwords that ended up in transcripts, and can shred them in place.

What is your team's agent bill hiding?

That was my own bill, one night: an eight-hour session whose subagents and streamed tokens were never counted. Glassbox is the tool I wished I'd had. Your team's bill may have the same blind spots.

The tool is free. The reading isn't. Your developers run one command that strips every prompt, file and result; I read what's left and send back where the money goes, the five patterns that cost most, the fixes, and a re-run thirty days later that proves whether they held.

On a subscription instead of a key? Then the bill is flat and the limit is the budget. The same waste brings the wall closer, and I report it as a share of your usage, not dollars you'll never see. How the audit reads a plan.

About

An operator who learned to build.

I started on a shop floor in Adelaide at fifteen, selling cameras and watches, and became the youngest supervisor the firm had at seventeen. Then Paris for a master's, and ten years in Europe buying and selling oil to clients in South America and Asia — where the number on the page was the whole conversation.

Back home I ran operations as a general manager twice, and spent years under the bonnet beside a qualified mechanic. Now I build software, and I build it in the open, because that is the only way I know to earn trust from people who have never met me.

I'm trying to become the best software developer in Adelaide and Australia. This page is the evidence so far.
