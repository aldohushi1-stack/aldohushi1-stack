Selling the paid spots on [aldo.today](https://aldo.today): one agent per suburb, one per council, and four local trades per council.
All copy here is a **draft**. Aldo sends it, in his name, when he says go.

Files:

- `README.md`: the offer, who to hit, the order to hit them in, and the rules
- `scripts.md`: walk-in, phone, email, LinkedIn, follow-ups, objections, close
- `councils.csv`: the 19 councils, the day each one's letters go up, prices and open spots
- `pipeline.csv`: one row per business you contact (blank, fill it in as you go)

Money never buys a number, a rank or a page; that's in the rules, and it's the trust the agent is renting.

## The ache (what they're really buying)

**Agents:** they don't want another lead source; they want to be *the* name for their patch.
When a seller in Henley Beach looks up what their house is worth, they meet a number and then a name, and the agent wants that name to be theirs.
That's **status, plus the relief of getting there first**, before the agent down the road does.

**Brokers, conveyancers, surveyors, builders:** they don't want ads; they want to be standing next to the numbers
at the moment someone works out they can afford it, or that their block might split.

## The hook: the letters calendar

One council's "Letters from the suburbs" goes up each day until 28 October (dates in `councils.csv`).
Each letter gives you a real, fresh reason to contact that council's agents **on that day**:
"your suburb got its letter today, here's the link."
Don't invent a deadline; the calendar already gives you one.

Letters that go up on a weekend (Charles Sturt and Onkaparinga this weekend, then 17–18 and 24–25 Oct) roll to the Monday.
So Monday 12 Oct is Charles Sturt + Onkaparinga + Port Adelaide Enfield, and that's the busy day.

Daily rhythm, Mon–Fri:

1. **Morning:** open the council whose letters went up today. Pick 5 agents who actually sell there:
   look at recent sold boards and the agency sites. Add them to `pipeline.csv`.
2. **Mid-morning:** walk in to 2–3 offices in person (Charles Sturt first, since it's home turf in Tennyson).
3. **Afternoon:** email or LinkedIn the rest, with the suburb link in the first line.
4. **Day 3:** one follow-up. **Day 8:** the last one. Then leave them alone.
5. Once a council's agents are done, hit its broker, conveyancer, surveyor and builder (one each).

Target per day: **10 touches, 2 walk-ins.** Over the 13 working days left in the calendar that's roughly 130 conversations.
If 1 in 15 says yes, that's about 9 spots.

## Who to go after first

1. **Agents with a sold board in the suburb this quarter.** They already care about that suburb's number.
2. **Small independent agencies** (1–3 offices). One decision-maker, and the office patch fits them best.
3. **High-sales suburbs**, where the page gets searched: Henley Beach (70 sales a year), the big Onkaparinga and Port Adelaide Enfield suburbs.
4. **Your own patch:** Tennyson, Grange, Henley Beach, Seaton, West Beach. You're the local, so say so.

Franchise principal or office manager → pitch the **agency bundle** or **office patch**.
Single agent → pitch **one suburb at $49**, and let it grow from there.

## Rules (read once, keep to them)

- **No numbers you can't show.** Traffic, enquiries and rankings come from the public ledger or the page itself, or they don't get said.
- **Spam Act 2003:** cold email to an agent's *published* work address about their work is generally fine (inferred consent).
  Every email names you, gives your contact details, and has a line like "Not for you? Reply 'no' and I won't email again." Honour it the same day.
- **Do Not Call Register:** some mobiles are registered. Call **office landlines** or walk in; only call a mobile the agent gave you or that has already replied.
- **You're not an agent and you don't earn from sales.** That's a selling point, so say it plainly. Never let it sound like a referral fee: the agent pays for the spot, not per sale or per lead.
- **No fake scarcity.** "Nobody has Henley Beach yet" is true today; check `/advertise` before you say it.
- **Money up front:** you invoice them, they don't invoice you. If anyone "partners" with you by asking you to pay first, it's a scam: walk away.
- **Drafts only.** Nothing here goes out until Aldo sends it.
